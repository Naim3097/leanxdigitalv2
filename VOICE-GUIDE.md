# Voice guide

How Lean X Digital sounds on leanxdigital.io. It sits on top of CONTENT-GUIDE.md: facts, SEO rules, banned words
and the no em dash rule all still apply in full.

## Where it comes from

In November 2026 we studied the copy on ploy.ai (its homepage, feature pages and audience pages) because the owner
likes how it reads: benefits before features, casual, a little witty, never trying too hard. We took the principles,
not the wording. What makes that writing work:

1. Wit grows with type size. A big headline may carry a wry line; the small text under it explains plainly.
2. Every playful line is followed straight away by a plain one that names the thing.
3. The product or the team does the work, and the reader keeps the result.
4. Results get the big type. Feature names become small labels.
5. Short sentences, with a long one that explains followed by a short one that lands the point.
6. Proof comes from names, steps and how things work, not from adjectives.
7. The scariest worry is answered before the final button.
8. Buttons start with a verb and never joke.
9. The search layer stays literal: titles, descriptions, structured data and opening sentences.
10. A few running images, called back once, tie a page together.

## The voice in one paragraph

We sound like a friendly specialist talking to a busy shop owner: the result first, then how it works, in short,
plain sentences, with a light line that shows we know their day. We never oversell, and we stay plain around
prices, payments, data and promises.

## Principles

| Principle | What it means for our readers |
| --- | --- |
| Result first, then how, then specifics | Owners about to spend money ask "what do I get?" before "how?" |
| We work, you decide | Our team or Nexova does the work. The reader chooses, and agrees the price before work starts |
| One light touch per section, then a literal line | Second-language readers can skip the joke and lose nothing |
| Show the work | Names, tools, steps and timings convince; praise doesn't. Until we publish case studies, checkable detail is our proof |
| Short and plain | Easier in a second language, and easier for AI tools to quote |
| Answer the worry beside the claim | Who decides, what it costs, what the limit is |
| Keep the search layer literal | Titles, H1 keywords, definitions, FAQ questions and Key facts never carry a joke |
| Same words for the same job | One label per action, each line once a page |

Keep Nexova's automation claims exactly as strong as they are. Stronger claims wait for proof.

## Headlines that keep SEO

- Plain and keyword-bearing: page titles, H1s ("We turn traffic into sales." on home), entity and service names,
  definition sentences after product and service H1s, Key facts, contact details, FAQ questions phrased the way
  customers search, and meta descriptions (120 to 165 characters, keywords kept).
- The wit goes in lines after the definition: ledes, body, cards, headings that carry no protected keyword,
  microcopy, the 404 and the contact band.
- Keyword first, twist second: "How to start with Nexova: paste a link, press Build".
- A playful heading needs a plain label or next sentence that names the topic.
- No jokes in anything that feeds structured data, llms.txt or share images (definitions, summaries, categories,
  feature lines, audience lines, FAQs, Key facts, bios).
- FAQ headings read "Questions about" plus the topic. Answers start with the direct answer: Yes, No, or the term.

## Sentence craft

- Body sentences average 8 to 14 words, none over 25. Headings 3 to 8 words, card lines under 15, buttons 1 to 4.
- A long sentence explains how; the short one after it says what that means.
- Fragments are fine in labels, bullets and the odd closer ("Free to start."), never in definitions.
- One question per section at most, and only a real one ("Selling online?").
- Everyday contractions (we'll, don't, isn't, you're) are fine, except in definitions, Key facts, prices and legal lines.
- "You" in nearly every paragraph. "We" or "our team" is Lean X Digital; Nexova is the subject of its own work.
- For second-language readers: subject and verb early, one idea per sentence, everyday verbs, jargon explained on
  first use ("conversions, such as leads and sales").

## Humour

Devices we use, each followed by a literal line: a familiar scene ("Lots of likes, no sales?"), a two-beat turn
("Clicks and followers are nice. Customers who pay are nicer."), a short aside ("No staring at an empty chat."), a
callback to a line earlier on the page.

Budget: at most one per section, never two sections in a row. Home 4, Nexova 3, each service page 2, the 404 2,
every other page 1. Never the same joke on two pages.

Never near money (prices, fees, budgets, "Free to start", payments), data, legal lines, team credentials, contact
details, definitions, FAQs, Key facts, results we don't control (rankings, ad approvals, marketplace sales, AI
citations), anything a customer relies on (steps, timings, scope, what's included), or anyone's expense.

Trying too hard looks like: jokes in a row or in every heading, puns on our names, exclamation marks, emoji,
shouting capitals, slang (yep, nope, awesome, boss, no-brainer), American sales talk (act now, limited time,
guaranteed), and invented numbers, even as a joke.

For Malaysian readers, use scenes every seller knows (WhatsApp enquiries, 11.11 sales, payday, late-night
packing), said plainly. No idioms, sports, American pop culture or Manglish particles, which sound mocking from a
company. Festivals, religion, race, politics and looks are never the joke.

## Words

Our verbs: make, run, check, fix, build, track, sync. The reader's: pick, choose, agree, decide, reply, sell.
Nouns: orders, customers, enquiries, stock, sales, fees, and the platforms by name.

Retired: "priced to the job" (an idiom; say "You agree the price for each job before work starts"), "handles its
SEO" outside Nexova's protected definition (say "works on its SEO"), "interested to work with" (say "I'd like to ask
about working with you"), and promised results such as "keeps bringing in customers while you sleep".

## Microcopy

- Buttons: a verb, then the next step, in 1 to 4 words. No jokes, no "Submit", "Click here" or "Learn more". One
  label per action: every nexova.my button says "Build on nexova.my".
- Labels, eyebrows, pills, captions and alt text: plain and literal. Sample data is marked as sample.
- Badges: one true fact each. No "best", "trusted by", awards or ratings.
- Pre-filled WhatsApp messages: the customer's polite voice ("Hi Lean X Digital! ..."), ending with a slot for
  their business or link.

## Before you publish copy

- [ ] The reader's result leads each section, and we or Nexova do the work.
- [ ] Light touches stay within the page's budget and outside the no-joke zones.
- [ ] No new or stronger claims. Protected items (titles, H1 keywords, definitions, FAQ questions, Key facts, names,
      contact details) are unchanged.
- [ ] Plain English: short sentences, sentence case, Malaysian spelling, no idioms, slang, em dashes or banned words.
- [ ] One label per action, and tight lines (hero, pills, panel titles, buttons) still fit.
- [ ] Read it aloud: would you say every line to a client?
